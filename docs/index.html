Monte Carlo Stock Simulator

10,000 possible futures for a stock, from two engines. Then an exam: put the simulator at ~180 past dates and check whether its “90%” bands catch reality 90% of the time.

What it shows

GBM (the model under Black–Scholes) draws each day from a bell curve calibrated on the last 5 years. The block bootstrap assumes no shape: it resamples real 21-day blocks, so crashes come back, and in clusters.

Both agree on the size of the risk. GBM gets the shape wrong: real returns are fat-tailed, mostly calmer than the bell curve says and sometimes far wilder, so its central bands are too wide. A VaR backtest alone barely notices. Checking the whole distribution does.

Correctness checks: simulated GBM matches its closed-form lognormal within sampling error, and on data that really is a bell curve the exam scores ≈90%. The backtest has no look-ahead.

Data: Yahoo Finance, split- and dividend-adjusted. The public demo uses precomputed results for 5 stocks; run python3 server.py locally for any ticker. Not investment advice. · github.com/nachoie123/monte-carlo-simulator
